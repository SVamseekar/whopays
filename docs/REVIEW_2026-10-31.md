## Why the project took this shape

The October decisions rejected AegisServe because serving schedulers are crowded and a
simulator would supply weak evidence. A generic compression/adaptation pipeline was also
rejected because it lacked a distinct question. WhoPays instead connects the owner's
econometrics and assurance background to practical inference engineering: measure paired
language effects, separate quality from speed, attribute effects to serving-stack factors,
and test a specific allocation objective. Small dense models, fake quantization, CPU
statistics and resumable T4 runs make the question feasible under the stated compute plan.

These choices remain useful. Additional quantizers, model families and a polished dashboard
are lower priority than valid baselines and a falsifiable result. ParityQuant protects
degradation relative to each language's FP16 baseline; it does not promise equal absolute
accuracy across languages. K2 can still cancel the method work while preserving the audit.

## Literature check and narrowed claims

This is a targeted primary-source search, not proof of absence of competing work.

- [Calibrating Beyond English](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.223/) confirms that
  calibration-language coverage is existing work. Treat it as a replication baseline.
- [Lightweight but Linguistically Fair?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18343), submitted in
  October, finds model/method-dependent language effects and uncertainty-related decision
  changes. Do not assume the lower-resource language always degrades most.
- [FairQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23192v1) already studies group-aware mixed
  precision and fairness under bit budgets in medical imaging. The general idea of
  fairness-aware bit allocation is not new.
- [Colla-Q](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18131v1) uses minimax precision balancing across
  MoE experts. ParityQuant's distinction must be the language-level degradation objective
  and its evaluation, not the word minimax.
- [CoopQ/SPQE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15455) and
  [MixQuant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23047) cover layer interactions in precision
  allocation. An independent-layer sensitivity surrogate needs validation under its
  resulting allocation. The former reference was previously labelled IMPQ here.
- [Towards Dynamic KV-Cache Compression](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=0noVRtnzJt)
  has multilingual VisR-Bench analyses in the indexed primary text. OpenReview blocked
  full-text access during this review. Withdraw the broad claim that no multilingual
  KV analysis exists; read the complete paper before asserting a narrower distinction.
- [Speculative Decoding and the Curse of Multilinguality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30580)
  already tests language-dependent speculative efficiency. Keep this as prior art.

Under K4, retain the candidate composed-stack disparity audit, separate quality/speed
channels, serving-factor attribution, and the specific worst-language dense-LLM allocation
comparison. Novelty remains provisional. No evidence found here warrants abandoning the
audit; the overlaps do require narrower claims and explicit citations.

## Research improvements in priority order

1. **Separate eviction dose from sensitivity.** The existing proposed ratio-matched
   retention control is the strongest extension. Compare the fixed token budget with a
   common retained-context fraction on the same parallel items. This can distinguish
   increased eviction caused by token premium from greater sensitivity at a matched dose.
   Keep it exploratory unless adopted in a dated amendment before looking at effects.
2. **Condition instability on baseline uncertainty.** Derive FP16 answer margins from
   already logged Belebele letter log-probabilities; compare flip rates within uncertainty
   strata. This addresses the recent dialect result without adding another model or
   quantizer. Specify strata before treatment analysis and keep parallel-item pairing.
3. **Validate allocation out of sample.** Profiling FLORES devtest makes it an allocation
   development signal. Belebele/MGSM remain independent downstream outcomes; do not label
   NLL on those same profiled sentences as an untouched test. A deterministic split for
   profile selection versus NLL validation is a useful prospective amendment before M4.
   Then check whether the additive surrogate predicts the allocated model's actual loss.

These are recommendations, not an expansion of the confirmatory GPU grid in this pass.
Do not spend additional GPU time until K1 and measured throughput justify the plan.

## Protocol repairs and remaining blockers

The pairwise Oaxaca script covariate identified group exactly and would mechanically
assign the entire gap to endowment. The PRD and prospective preregistration now exclude
such covariates, and the implementation rejects any design whose column span contains the
group indicator. This preserves H3 as a testable descriptive question; it is not causal
identification of discrimination.

Before M2/M3, define valid frequentist interaction tests for H2/BH. The existing Bayesian
variational GLMM's credible intervals cannot be substituted for those p-values. Before
M4, define a sensitivity-independent mixed-width comparator at fractional bit budgets;
uniform precision cannot represent them. Before profiling resumptions, enforce the same
code/model/GPU/library provenance discipline used by the item-run harness.
